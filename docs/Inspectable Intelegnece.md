### Two-pillar mathematical grounding
EEV3 explicitly grounds itself in two formally significant problems from geometric measure theory and analytic number theory:

- **Kakeya–Besicovitch (geometric pillar):** A compact set in ℝⁿ containing a unit segment in every direction can have Lebesgue measure zero — established by A. S. Besicovitch, "On Kakeya's problem and a similar one," *Mathematische Zeitschrift* 27 (1928), pp. 312–320. The Kakeya conjecture asserts Hausdorff dimension n. PeAIce maps the *needle* to the *trust vector L*, the directional sphere Sⁿ⁻¹ to the *full relational sphere* (grief, code, medicine, ethics), and measure → 0 to *zero relational intrusion — presence without occupation*. The May 2026 documents acknowledge the 3-D resolution by Hong Wang (NYU Courant) and Joshua Zahl (UBC), "Volume estimates for unions of convex sets, and the Kakeya set conjecture in three dimensions," arXiv:2502.17655 (24 February 2025) — which Nets Katz of Rice University, quoted in Quanta Magazine on 14 March 2025, called "a once-in-a-century kind of result" — and the subsequent streamlined proof by Larry Guth, Hong Wang, and Joshua Zahl, "A streamlined proof of the Kakeya set conjecture in ℝ³," arXiv:2601.14411 (20 January 2026; abstract: "We present a streamlined and simplified proof of the Kakeya set conjecture in ℝ³"). The framework treats the higher-dimensional case as a deliberate structural analogy: the framework is active even while the formal proof is open in dimensions ≥ 4.
- **Riemann / zeta-line (spectral pillar):** Re(s) = ½ is the fixed point of the functional-equation map s → 1 − s — the only value that reflects to itself.
