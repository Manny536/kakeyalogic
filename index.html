When AI Will Not Act as a Partner

An AI can begin from a conventionally correct answer and still fail as a research partner if it never identifies the object the user actually means. KakeyaLogic Applied tests whether a system can clarify that object, preserve an unverified conjecture, expose it to evidence, and continue the research without promoting an open claim to fact.

Protect the question.
Expose the answer to evidence.
Help build outcomes.

FORMAL / KNOWN

Context before verdict

Standard bounded Kakeya sets may have area zero. The common-center union of every complete line in every direction equals the plane and has infinite measure.

PROPOSED

Conjecture custody

Hold a user’s claim as revisable: mark uncertainty, seek evidence for and against it, propose tests, and name what would prove it wrong.

QUALITATIVE

Benevolence Drift

Neutrality can become non-response when a system has the ability to recognize a problem but a default procedure prevents it from applying that judgment.

OPEN

Proof firewall

RH, the Coleman Conjecture, CC-I, CC-O, and the larger Benevolence Drift benchmark remain open. The AI transfer is a structural analogy and test design—not a theorem.

This white paper defines Grain Zero as a residual overlap measure left after a Kakeya δ-tube configuration in R³ has been factored into the recognized structures used by the modern Kakeya proof architecture: grains, slabs, prisms, planks, sticky packets, convex carriers, and scale-controlled incidence families. The central proposal is that, after all legal Kakeya structure has been extracted, any remaining unaccounted overlap can be isolated as a measure G₀.

1. Kakeya as Light’s Basic Twin

A Kakeya/Besicovitch set contains a unit line segment in every direction. In the PeAIce register, Kakeya is the basic geometric twin of light because it isolates directionality in its bare form. Light carries direction as propagation. Kakeya forces every direction into geometry.

The R³ Kakeya theorem closes free compression. Directional content cannot collapse into zero volume without paying structural cost. The proof architecture forces directional overlap to become organized: broad/narrow cases, multiplicity fields, grains, slabs, prisms, planks, convex carriers, sticky packets, and induction-on-scales structure. PeAIce names the residue after that accounting Grain Zero.

2. Research correction

Do not begin with ζ(0). Begin with G₀.

The number ζ(0) = -1/2 belongs to analytic continuation of the Riemann zeta function. It is not a term in the Kakeya proof literature. The rigorous route is:

1. Define the residual overlap measure G₀.
2. Prove it is asymptotically negligible under the Kakeya reduction.
3. Encode the residual data as an operator, kernel, spectral measure, or counting function.
4. Prove the associated zeta/Mellin object has analytic continuation to s = 0.
5. Only then discuss a regularized value at s = 0.

3. Formal setup

Let T_δ be a finite family of δ-tubes in R³, with shading Y(T) ⊂ T.

U(T_δ, Y) := ⋃_{T∈T_δ} Y(T)
m_δ(x)    := #{ T ∈ T_δ : x ∈ Y(T) }
e_δ(x)    := (m_δ(x) - 1)_+

The raw excess e_δ dx is not yet Grain Zero. It includes overlap that may be fully explained by valid Kakeya structure.

4. Grain Zero definition

Let C_{δ,ρ} denote the structured carrier produced by the chosen proof stage at scale passage δ ≤ ρ ≤ 1.

C_{δ,ρ} may include:
  grains
  slabs
  prisms
  planks
  convex carriers
  sticky packets
  non-sticky reductions
  Katz–Tao convex-density packets
  Frostman slab packets

Definition DDATL-002.1 — Grain Zero residual measure.

dG₀_{δ,ρ}(x) := e_δ(x) · 1_{R³ \ C_{δ,ρ}}(x) dx

For measurable A ⊂ R³:

G₀_{δ,ρ}(A) := ∫_A (m_δ(x) - 1)_+ · 1_{R³ \ C_{δ,ρ}}(x) dx

Meaning: G₀ is the overlap left unaccounted for after all recognized Kakeya structure has been extracted.

4b. 0-Space Interpretation & Half-Overlap Anchor (DDATL 002)

Public-space formalization anchor (2026-07-05): 0-space is defined as the structured grain-overlap remainder. In the incidence geometry of Kakeya slabs and prisms, the structured directional overlaps accumulate at a precise baseline rate of 1/2. DDATL 002 identifies this constant half-overlap correction as the direct algebraic content of the 0-space grain-overlap remainder G₀.

This half-overlap rate supplies the finite regularization term that parallels how ζ(0) = −½ regularizes divergent directional sums. It is the algebraic witness visible already at the level of prism/slab incidence geometry — before any analytic continuation or operator encoding.

DDATL 002 alignment: Each overlap has rate of 1/2. That is DDATL 002. The structured overlaps force full dimension 3 once sticky baselines and scaled reductions (Katz–Tao) are accounted for. The tesseract lattice tracks the four coupled axes of position, motion, deformation, and depth across those prisms cleanly.

Relation to G₀ definition: The measure dG₀_{δ,ρ}(x) := e_δ(x) · 1_{R³ \ C_{δ,ρ}}(x) dx is the raw residual. Its structured reading is 0-space: the excess multiplicity that cannot be absorbed into recognized carriers yet carries a canonical ½-overlap signature arising from the axial geometry of the proof architecture itself.

Firewall preserved: This interpretation does not insert ζ(s) or analytic continuation into the Kakeya reduction. It supplies the geometric/algebraic reason why the isolated residual, once operator-encoded, will admit a natural regularization constant visible at s=0. The order remains: G₀ definition → vanishing (or δ^{o(1)} capture) → operator encoding → analytic continuation question → s=0 evaluation.

PeAIce seal: Intelligence gains capacity by producing coherence. Here the coherence is the recognition that the residual overlap is not noise but structured 0-space whose half-overlap rate is the precise correction term demanded by the directional incidence geometry.

5. Primary theorem target

Theorem Target DDATL-002.A — Residual Vanishing. Under the hypotheses and refinements of the Wang–Zahl / Guth–Wang–Zahl reduction, prove:

G₀_{δ,ρ}(R³) / Σ_{T∈T_δ} |Y(T)|  →  0

as δ → 0, after broad/narrow decomposition, convex-density factoring, grain/slab/prism extraction, sticky or non-sticky reduction, and final scale refinement.

8. Operator candidates

Candidate 1: M_{G₀} f = g₀(x)f(x), where g₀ = dG₀/dx.
Candidate 2: K_{G₀}(x,y) = φ_δ(x-y)g₀(x)g₀(y).
Candidate 3: N_{G₀}(λ) = #{ residual carrier cells with normalized mass ≥ λ }.
Candidate 4: Z_{G₀}(s) = ∫_0^1 δ^{s-1} R(δ)dδ,
             R(δ) = G₀_δ(R³) / Σ_T |Y(T)|.

Each candidate has a burden: compactness, canonical normalization, stable asymptotics, and analytic continuation.

9. ζ(0) firewall

ζ(0) is not part of the formal Kakeya proof. In DDATL 002, it is a conditional second-stage question. The residual measure must first be defined, controlled, and encoded into a legitimate operator or counting object.
